# Philippine Labor Law (DOLE) - Employee and HR Quick Guide

This guide summarises common rules from the Labor Code of the Philippines (Presidential Decree 442, as amended) and DOLE issuances in plain language. It is general guidance, not legal advice. Company policy may be more generous than the legal minimum, in which case the company policy applies. Wage rates, contribution tables, and holiday proclamations change regularly, so confirm current figures with HR or DOLE (hotline 1349, dole.gov.ph).

## 1. Hours of Work and Rest
- Normal hours of work: not more than 8 hours a day.
- Meal break: at least 60 minutes, not counted as working time. A shorter break of not less than 20 minutes may be allowed in specific cases under DOLE rules.
- Weekly rest day: at least 24 consecutive hours after every 6 consecutive normal work days.
- Overtime: work beyond 8 hours a day. Overtime must be paid and cannot be offset by undertime on another day.
- Telecommuting (RA 11165): employees who work from home are entitled to the same pay, benefits, rest days, and working hours as on-site employees.

## 2. Overtime, Rest Day, Holiday and Night Pay
Premium rates (as a share of the daily or hourly rate):
- Overtime on an ordinary working day: plus 25% of the hourly rate (125%).
- Work on a rest day or special non-working day: 130% of the daily rate for the first 8 hours. Overtime on those days: plus 30% of the hourly rate on top of the 130% rate.
- Work on a regular holiday: 200% of the daily rate for the first 8 hours. Overtime on a regular holiday: plus 30% of the hourly rate on top of the 200% rate.
- Work on a regular holiday that is also the rest day: 260%.
- Work on a special non-working day that is also the rest day: 150%.
- A special non-working day that is not worked: "no work, no pay", unless company policy or a collective bargaining agreement provides otherwise.
- A regular holiday that is not worked: the employee receives 100% of the daily wage (holiday pay), provided the employee was present or on paid leave on the day before the holiday.
- Special working days: paid at the ordinary rate for work performed.
- Night shift differential: at least 10% of the regular hourly wage for each hour worked between 10:00 PM and 6:00 AM.
Managerial employees, field personnel, members of the family of the employer, domestic workers, and others listed in the Labor Code are exempt from overtime, holiday pay, and similar premium pay. Confirm eligibility with HR.

## 3. 13th Month Pay (Presidential Decree 851)
- Who: all rank-and-file employees, regardless of their position, designation, or employment status and regardless of how their wages are paid, who worked at least one month during the calendar year.
- Amount: not less than one-twelfth (1/12) of the total basic salary earned during the calendar year. Basic salary does not include allowances and monetary benefits that are not part of the basic salary, such as cost-of-living allowance, overtime pay, premium pay, and night differential.
- Formula: total basic salary actually earned from January to December divided by 12.
- Deadline: paid not later than December 24 of each year. Employers may give half in mid-year (before the opening of classes) and the balance by December 24.
- Employees who resign or are terminated before year-end are entitled to a pro-rated 13th month pay for the months worked.
- Managerial employees are not covered by the law, but company policy may grant it.

## 4. Service Incentive Leave (SIL)
- Every employee who has rendered at least one year of service is entitled to five (5) days of paid service incentive leave per year.
- Unused SIL is convertible to cash at the end of the year, based on the salary rate at that time.
- Establishments regularly employing fewer than ten employees, those already granting at least 5 days of paid vacation leave, and managerial employees and field personnel are exempt. Company vacation or sick leave that gives at least 5 days of paid leave satisfies this requirement.

## 5. Statutory Leaves
- Maternity leave (RA 11210, Expanded Maternity Leave Law): 105 days of paid leave for live childbirth, regardless of whether the delivery is normal or caesarean. Solo parents get an additional 15 days. Miscarriage or emergency termination of pregnancy: 60 days. A female worker may extend by 30 days without pay. She may allocate up to 7 days of her leave to the child's father or an alternate caregiver. Benefits are paid through SSS for covered employees, with the employer paying any salary difference as required by law.
- Paternity leave (RA 8187): 7 calendar days with full pay for married male employees for the first four deliveries of the legitimate spouse with whom he cohabits.
- Solo parent leave (RA 8972 as amended by RA 11861): 7 working days of paid parental leave per year for a solo parent who has rendered at least six months of service.
- Leave for victims of violence against women and their children (RA 9262): up to 10 days of paid leave, extendable when needed.
- Special leave benefit for women (RA 9710, Magna Carta of Women): up to two months with full pay for gynecological surgery, subject to conditions (at least 6 months of continuous service in the last 12 months).
- Vacation and sick leave beyond the SIL are not mandated by law and depend on company policy or contract.

## 6. Wages, Deductions and Final Pay
- Minimum wage is set per region by the Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Boards through wage orders. Check the latest wage order for your region.
- Wages must be paid at least once every two weeks or twice a month at intervals not exceeding 16 days, in legal tender.
- Deductions from wages are allowed only when authorised by law (for example SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, withholding tax) or with the employee's written authorisation for lawful purposes.
- Final pay (DOLE Labor Advisory 06-20): released within 30 days from the date of separation or termination, unless a more favourable company policy or practice exists. It includes unpaid salary, pro-rated 13th month pay, cash conversion of unused leave credits where applicable, and other benefits due.
- Certificate of Employment: must be issued within three (3) days from the employee's request.
- Service charges (RA 11360): 100% of service charges collected in covered establishments (such as hotels and restaurants) are distributed to covered employees.

## 7. Government-Mandated Contributions
Employers must register employees and withhold and remit contributions to SSS (RA 11199), PhilHealth (RA 11223, Universal Health Care Act), and Pag-IBIG/HDMF (RA 9679) on time. Contribution rates and salary brackets are revised periodically, so refer to the latest circulars from each agency. Employers also withhold income tax on compensation under BIR rules. Failure to remit may result in penalties.

## 8. Employment Status
- Probationary employment: may not exceed six (6) months unless covered by an apprenticeship agreement. The employer must explain the standards for regularization to the employee at the time of engagement. An employee allowed to work after the probationary period becomes a regular employee.
- Regular employment: employees engaged to perform work usually necessary or desirable in the usual business of the employer, or those who have rendered at least one year of service in the same activity.
- Project, seasonal, and casual employees: have specific rules under the Labor Code. Casual employees who render at least one year of service become regular.
- Fixed-term arrangements and contractualisation: "labor-only contracting" is prohibited. Legitimate contracting requires a registered contractor and compliance with DOLE Department Order 174-17.

## 9. Termination of Employment
Valid grounds:
- Just causes (employee fault): serious misconduct, willful disobedience of lawful orders, gross and habitual neglect of duties, fraud or willful breach of trust, commission of a crime against the employer or the employer's family or representatives, and analogous causes.
- Authorised causes (business or health reasons): installation of labor-saving devices, redundancy, retrenchment to prevent losses, closure or cessation of business, and disease of the employee when continued employment is prohibited by law or harmful to health.
Procedure (due process):
- Just cause - the "twin-notice rule": (1) a written notice to explain that states the specific acts or omissions and the company rule violated; (2) a reasonable chance (at least 5 calendar days) to answer, with a hearing or conference where requested; (3) a written notice of decision stating the reasons.
- Authorised cause: written notice to the employee and to DOLE at least 30 days before the effective date.
Separation pay:
- Redundancy or labor-saving devices: at least one month pay, or one month pay for every year of service, whichever is higher.
- Retrenchment or closure not caused by serious losses: at least one-half month pay for every year of service, or one month pay, whichever is higher.
- Disease: at least one month pay, or one-half month pay for every year of service, whichever is greater.
- Termination for just cause generally carries no separation pay, though financial assistance may be granted in some cases as a matter of equity.
An employee who is dismissed without just cause or without due process may file a case for illegal dismissal, which can lead to reinstatement and back wages.
Resignation: an employee who resigns without a just cause must give the employer at least 30 days' written notice, unless the contract or policy provides otherwise. Resignation for just causes (for example serious insult by the employer) can be immediate. Resignation does not entitle the employee to separation pay unless the contract, policy, or a CBA provides it.

## 10. Retirement (RA 7641, Article 302 of the Labor Code)
- Under a retirement plan or CBA, the stated age applies. Otherwise an employee may opt to retire at age 60 or above (but below 65) with at least five years of service, and retirement is compulsory at 65.
- Minimum retirement pay: at least one-half month salary for every year of service, where a fraction of at least six months counts as one whole year. "One-half month salary" is computed as 22.5 days: 15 days salary, plus 2.5 days (one-twelfth of the 13th month pay), plus 5 days of service incentive leave.

## 11. Workplace Conduct and Safety
- Sexual harassment (RA 7877 and the Safe Spaces Act, RA 11313): employers must prevent and address harassment, set up a committee on decorum and investigation, and act on complaints promptly.
- Occupational safety and health (RA 11058 and DOLE DO 198-18): employers must provide a safe workplace, safety officers, safety training, and personal protective equipment, and report work-related accidents and illnesses.
- Anti-discrimination and equal pay for equal work: discrimination in terms of employment on the basis of sex, age (RA 10911), or other protected characteristics is prohibited.
- Employee data (see the Data Privacy Act guide): HR must protect employee records, and employees have the right to access and correct their personal data.

## 12. Disciplinary Process and Complaints
- Employees should first raise concerns with their supervisor or HR using the company grievance process.
- DOLE offers the Single Entry Approach (SEnA), a 30-day conciliation-mediation service for labor disputes, before cases are formally filed with the National Labor Relations Commission (NLRC).
- Employees may call the DOLE hotline 1349 or visit the nearest DOLE Field or Regional Office.
