# Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173) - Employee Guide

This guide summarises the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173), its Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR), and National Privacy Commission (NPC) issuances in plain language for employees. It is general guidance, not legal advice. Confirm specific cases with the company Data Protection Officer (DPO).

## 1. Key Terms
- Personal information: any information from which the identity of an individual is apparent or can reasonably be ascertained, for example name, address, email, phone number, photo, employee ID.
- Sensitive personal information: race, ethnic origin, marital status, age, color, religious, philosophical or political affiliation, health, education, genetic or sexual life, legal proceedings, and government-issued identifiers (SSS, GSIS, PhilHealth, TIN, Pag-IBIG numbers, passport, driver's licence), and information marked classified by law.
- Privileged information: data that is privileged under the Rules of Court and other laws.
- Data subject: the individual whom the personal data is about (for us: customers, employees, job applicants, suppliers' contacts).
- Personal Information Controller (PIC): decides what data is collected and why. Personal Information Processor (PIP): processes data on behalf of a PIC (for example a payroll or cloud vendor).
- Processing: any operation on personal data, including collecting, recording, storing, updating, using, sharing, blocking, erasing, or destroying it.
- National Privacy Commission (NPC): the government agency that administers and enforces the Act.

## 2. General Privacy Principles
Personal data must be processed following three principles:
- Transparency: the data subject knows what data is collected, why, how it is used, and who receives it (a privacy notice).
- Legitimate purpose: the purpose is not contrary to law, morals, or public policy and is declared in advance.
- Proportionality: only data that is adequate, relevant, suitable, and necessary for the declared purpose is collected, and it is not kept longer than needed.

## 3. Lawful Processing
Personal information may be processed when at least one of the following applies: the data subject gave consent; processing is needed to fulfil a contract with the data subject; it is needed to comply with a legal obligation; it protects vital interests such as life and health; it responds to a national emergency or public order; it is needed for a public authority's mandate; or it serves the legitimate interests of the PIC or a third party, except where overridden by the data subject's fundamental rights.
Sensitive personal information is stricter. It may generally be processed only with the specific consent of the data subject, or where a law or regulation allows it, or in other cases listed in Section 13 of the Act (for example protecting life and health, or establishing, exercising or defending legal claims).
Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, and evidenced by written, electronic, or recorded means. Silence, pre-ticked boxes, and inactivity are not consent.

## 4. Rights of the Data Subject
Individuals have the right to:
- Be informed whether and how their personal data is being processed.
- Object to processing, including direct marketing and automated decision-making.
- Access their personal data and information on its sources, recipients, and purposes.
- Rectify (correct) inaccurate or outdated data.
- Erasure or blocking when the data is incomplete, outdated, false, unlawfully obtained, used for an unauthorised purpose, or no longer necessary.
- Be indemnified for damages caused by inaccurate, false, unlawfully obtained, or unauthorised use of their data.
- Data portability: obtain a copy of their data in a structured, commonly used format where processing is done electronically.
- File a complaint with the NPC.
Employees who receive a request from a customer or colleague to see, correct, or delete data must forward it to the DPO immediately and must not ignore or refuse it on their own.

## 5. Employee Responsibilities (Do and Do Not)
Do:
- Collect only the personal data needed for the task, and tell the person why you need it.
- Keep customer and employee data inside approved company systems (CRM, ERP, HR system, approved file shares).
- Lock your screen, use a clean desk, and shred or securely dispose of printed documents containing personal data.
- Use strong, unique passwords and multi-factor authentication where available.
- Share data internally only on a need-to-know basis, and only with approved vendors that have a data sharing or outsourcing agreement.
- Send files containing personal data through approved, access-controlled channels and use password protection or encryption.
Do not:
- Send personal data using personal email, personal cloud storage, or consumer messaging apps.
- Copy customer or employee lists to USB drives or personal devices.
- Post photos of IDs, quotations, delivery receipts, or screens showing personal data on social media or chat groups.
- Use customer data for a purpose other than the one declared (for example marketing) without consent.
- Share your login credentials.

## 6. Personal Data Breach Response
A personal data breach is a breach of security that leads to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to personal data. Examples: a lost laptop, an email sent to the wrong recipient, a ransomware attack, an exposed shared folder, or a stolen phone with company email.
What an employee must do:
- Report it immediately to the DPO and the IT Security team. Do not wait to confirm how serious it is, and do not try to hide or fix it quietly.
- Preserve evidence (emails, logs, device) and follow instructions from IT.
What the company must do (NPC Circular 16-03):
- Notify the NPC and the affected data subjects within 72 hours from knowledge of, or reasonable belief that, a breach occurred, when sensitive personal information or information that can be used for identity fraud is involved and there is a real risk of serious harm.
- Provide notification details: nature of the breach, data involved, measures taken, and DPO contact.
- Document all breaches, including those that are not notified, and submit an annual summary report to the NPC.
Concealing a security breach is itself a punishable offence under the Act.

## 7. Data Protection Officer and Accountability
- Every PIC and PIP must appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO). The DPO monitors compliance, handles data subject requests and complaints, trains staff, and is the contact point with the NPC.
- Organisations must maintain a privacy management programme: privacy notices, privacy impact assessments (PIA) for new systems or projects that process personal data, policies on retention and disposal, security measures (organisational, physical, technical), and breach procedures.
- NPC registration of data processing systems applies to PICs and PIPs that meet the criteria set by NPC issuances (for example employing 250 or more persons, or processing sensitive personal information of 1,000 or more individuals, or processing that is likely to pose a risk to data subjects). Registration status is handled by the DPO.

## 8. Security Measures
The Act requires reasonable and appropriate organisational, physical, and technical measures, such as: access control by role, encryption of data at rest and in transit, regular backups, patching, firewalls and endpoint protection, logging and monitoring, security incident procedures, and confidentiality commitments from staff.
When selling or supporting IT products (servers, storage, firewalls, backup systems) for customers, remember that company staff who can access customer systems or data are bound by confidentiality and by the customer's data sharing or outsourcing agreement.

## 9. Retention and Disposal
Keep personal data only as long as necessary for the declared purpose or as required by law (for example tax and labor records). After that, delete or destroy it securely: shred paper, and use secure erase or physical destruction for storage devices that held personal data. Returning or decommissioning customer equipment must follow the secure-wipe procedure agreed with the customer.

## 10. Specific Situations
- CCTV: allowed for security with a clear purpose, visible notices, limited access to footage, and limited retention. Do not use cameras in toilets, locker rooms, or similar private areas.
- Employee records: HR may process employee data for hiring, payroll, benefits, and legal obligations. Medical and government ID data are sensitive and need stricter access.
- Customer quotations and sales orders: contain contact details and possibly government IDs for credit checks. Share only with people who need them for the transaction.
- Marketing emails: need a lawful basis and an easy opt-out. Do not add customers to a mailing list without a lawful basis.
- Taking photos at events: tell attendees and respect objections.
- Third-party vendors: no personal data is shared until a data sharing agreement or outsourcing agreement is in place.
- Cross-border transfers: the PIC remains accountable and must use contractual or other reasonable means to ensure comparable protection.

## 11. Penalties
The Act imposes both imprisonment and fines. Examples: unauthorised processing of personal information carries imprisonment of 1 to 3 years and a fine of PHP 500,000 to PHP 2,000,000; for sensitive personal information, 3 to 6 years and PHP 500,000 to PHP 4,000,000. Concealing a security breach carries imprisonment of 1 year and 6 months to 5 years and a fine of PHP 500,000 to PHP 1,000,000. Responsible officers of a company can be held personally liable. Individuals may also claim damages. Penalties and details should be verified against the text of the Act.

## 12. Where to Get Help
- Report incidents and questions to the company DPO and the IT Security team (contact details are on the intranet).
- Individuals who believe their rights were violated can file a complaint with the National Privacy Commission (privacy.gov.ph).
